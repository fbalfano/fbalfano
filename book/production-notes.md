## Back-cover copy (short version, ~120 words)

Almost no one is caught the way the movies promise. There is rarely a surprise
witness or a confession that shatters on the stand. What catches a killer, nearly
every time, is smaller and quieter: the one thing he forgot he left behind.

A cell tower that puts a phone on a lonely road. A Fitbit still counting steps for a
woman her husband has already reported dead. A search for "what happens when you
die," typed minutes after a murder. In forty-three true cases from Canadian and
American courts, Frank Alfano traces the single thread of evidence that undid each
killer, and tells you the exact court decision to look it up yourself.

Every case is real. Every citation checks out. Everything remembers now.

## Book description (long version, for KDP / back matter / catalog, ~200 words)

We imagine murder as a locked-room puzzle solved by a genius in the final act. The
truth is closer to accounting. A person who takes a life has to move through the
world before and after doing it, and the world keeps records. What our own century
has added is only the sheer number of them: the phones that log where we stood, the
wristbands that count our heartbeats, the cars that remember our speed, the search
bars that never forget what we asked.

*How Not to Get Away With Murder* collects forty-three true cases in which a single,
understandable thread of evidence caught a killer, from cell-tower pings and Fitbit
data to gunshot residue, blood spatter, black boxes, and confessions coaxed out by
undercover police. Each chapter is a self-contained story you can read in any order,
and each is anchored to a real appellate court decision, with the citation and a free
link so you never have to take the author's word for any of it.

It is also honest about the other edge of the blade: the cases where the science was
wrong, the evidence was thrown out, or an innocent person was freed. Everything
remembers now. This is the story of what that means.
