# Gaps & Future Chapters

Forensic techniques that have caught killers but do **not** yet have a Canadian
Court of Appeal or Supreme Court decision setting out the facts. Documented here
honestly so we don't fabricate an anchor - and so we know what to watch for.

---

## Forensic genetic genealogy (cold-case DNA-database hits)
No qualifying Canadian appellate decision exists. The leading Canadian cases
resolved before any completed appeal:
- **R. v. Marc-André Grenon** - murder of Guylaine Potvin (Jonquière, 2000;
  convicted 2024). Investigators traced the surname via Y-chromosome/genetic
  genealogy, then covertly collected his DNA from a discarded cup and straws at a
  movie theatre. First genetic-genealogy case tried in Quebec. He filed a notice
  of appeal in 2024 but **abandoned it** - only trial-level material exists.
- **Joseph George Sutherland** - 1983 Toronto murders of Erin Gilmour and Susan
  Tice; resolved by guilty plea.
- **Christine Jessop / Calvin Hoover** identification - Hoover was deceased; no
  contested ruling.

**How to handle:** a strong chapter, but sourced from trial records and
reporting, clearly labelled as not-yet-appellate. Or wait for a genetic-genealogy
case litigated to a Court of Appeal.

## Fitbit / smartwatch alibi-breakers
No verifiable Canadian appellate murder case. The landmark is **American** -
*State v. Dabate* (Connecticut), where the victim's Fitbit logged her moving
long after the husband claimed she'd been shot. Option: a "crossing the border"
sidebar on wearables if the book allows non-Canadian cases.

## Smart-home / IoT devices (Alexa, Echo, etc.)
No verifiable Canadian appellate murder case. Leading example is **American** -
*State v. Bates* (Arkansas, Amazon Echo). The closest Canadian "connected
device" story is Sandeson's own-apartment camera (Ch. 15).

## Automated licence-plate readers (ALPR)
No Canadian appellate **murder** decision found where ALPR was central. Reported
Canadian ALPR cases involve lesser matters (impaired driving, failing to stop,
customs). Likely a trial-level or U.S.-sourced sidebar if we want it.

## Deleted files / computer forensics - best story has no appeal
**R. v. Twitchell** (Edmonton "Dexter killer") is the textbook deleted-files
case: forensic examiners recovered a deleted document titled "SKConfessions"
describing the real murder of Johnny Altinger. But **Twitchell abandoned his
appeal** - only the trial decision (*R. v. Twitchell*, 2010 ABQB 666) exists, so
it fails the appellate rule. Garland (Ch. 13) covers search-history/hard-drive
forensics with a proper appellate anchor instead.

## A cautionary near-miss - when digital evidence is EXCLUDED
**R. v. Rafferty** (Tori Stafford murder): the accused's laptop searches were
**excluded** at trial as too prejudicial - so digital evidence did *not* convict
him. Not a "how they got caught" chapter, but a possible sidebar on admissibility
limits (appeal: 2017 ONCA 778).

## Leads chased (research pass, July 2026)

### ✅ DRAFTED - pulled, written, and integrated into the book
- **R. v. Vader**, **2019 ABCA 191** (Travis Vader, the McCann murders; SCC leave
  denied, file 38764). Thread: the victims' own **cell phone** (Vader called an ex
  on the McCanns' phone two hours after they vanished) plus DNA/blood/prints in the
  recovered SUV. Trial judge's erroneous murder convictions were vacated and
  **manslaughter** substituted; appeal dismissed. **→ now Chapter 16** (Part II),
  `book/chapters/ch16-vader-victims-phone.md`.
- **R. v. Comber**, **2026 MBCA 27** (William Comber, Hailey Dugay road shooting).
  Thread: **ballistics** cleared the obvious suspect (Paluk's bolt-action Remington
  did not fire the fatal shot) and revealed a hidden second shooter; Comber's
  Winchester was never recovered, so the fatal weapon's make could not be
  conclusively matched. Second-degree **murder set aside as unreasonable on appeal
  and manslaughter substituted**. **→ now Chapter 28** (Part V),
  `book/chapters/ch28-comber-ballistics.md`.

### 🔶 SIDEBAR-ONLY - citation corrected
- **R. v. Rafferty**, **2016 ONCA 816** (NOT "2017 ONCA 778", which is an
  unrelated case; Tori Stafford murder; conviction **affirmed**). The laptop
  searches were **excluded at trial** under Charter s. 8, so this is not a
  "caught by digital evidence" story - only a sidebar on suppressed digital
